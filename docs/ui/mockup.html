Red wine braised short ribs

Deeply browned ribs, a slow oven braise, and a sharp gremolata to cut through it. Most of the time is hands-off; the mash and gremolata fit inside the braise.

Made 6× Original recipeseriouseats.com(opens in a new tab)
Ready in about 4 h
4 servings
Tip

Salt the ribs the night before if you remember. Noticeably better crust. From your notes

  1. 1

    Brown the ribs

    Pat the ribs dry and season all over with the salt. Heat the oil in the Dutch oven over medium-high and brown the ribs in two batches, about 4 minutes per side. Move to a plate.

    20 min hands-onDutch oven
  2. 2

    Soften the vegetables

    Pour off all but 2 tbsp fat. Cook the onions and carrots until soft and browned at the edges, 8 minutes. Add garlic and tomato paste; stir 1 minute until the paste darkens.

    10 min hands-onafter step 1
  3. 3

    Deglaze and braise

    Add the wine, scraping up the browned bits, and simmer 5 minutes. Add stock, bay and thyme, return the ribs, cover, and braise at 160 °C until a knife slides through the meat.

    3 h in the oven · check at 2 h 30
    Timer · 3 hafter step 2
    Meanwhile, while it braises
    • 4Make the mash. Start 40 minutes before the braise ends.
    • 5Make the gremolata. Any time; it keeps for a day.
  4. 4

    Boil and mash the potatoes

    Cover the potatoes with cold salted water, bring to a boil and cook until tender, 20 minutes. Drain, steam dry 2 minutes, then mash with the butter and warm milk.

    25 min hands-onTimer · 20 minduring step 3
  5. 5

    Make the gremolata

    Mix the lemon zest, parsley and grated garlic with a pinch of salt.

    5 min hands-onduring step 3
  6. 6

    Reduce the sauce and serve

    Lift out the ribs and skim the fat. Boil the liquid until it coats a spoon, about 15 minutes. Serve ribs over mash with sauce spooned over and gremolata on top.

    15 min hands-onafter steps 3, 4, 5

  • Timers stay pinned and keep counting between steps. The braise runs while you make the mash.

  • Back and Next are big enough to hit with a floury knuckle. The screen stays awake.
